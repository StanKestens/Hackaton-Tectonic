The Inspiration: TIMeSynC

The Problem:

  • Bank data sits in many tables at different time resolutions (swipes by second, statements by month, products by year) .
  • Classic models flatten this data into hand-made features and lose the sequence of events .

The Solution:

  • Put every record from every channel into one time-sorted token stream .
  • On over 30M Capital One customers, TIMeSynC beats SASRec .
  • Recall@1 improves by +7.8% relative to SASRec .

Fully Implemented Components

We translated the paper directly into our KBC code architecture :

Flattening & Quantization

All channels become one stream of time, field name, field value, and product . Amounts become bins, so numbers act as tokens .

Time & Product Fusion

Embeddings keep track of "which field / which account" and capture point-in-time product ownership .

TimeAliBi Attention

Attention decays dynamically with the time gap between events .

Causal Masking

A prediction strictly only sees events before its own moment, preventing data leakage .

One Timeline In, Two Predictions Out

1. Encoder (Context)

  • Raw transactions (merchant, MCC, amount, type) .
  • Monthly spend per category, savings & investment balances .
  • App logins, searches, and product enrollments .

2. Decoder (Intents)

  • Cross-attention to context before the query time using TimeAliBi .
  • Outputs two heads: "Next intent" and "Needs in next 30 days" .

A small model (2 layers, 64 dimensions, 4 heads) trains in about 12 minutes on a laptop .

What We Added for KBC

  • 30-Day Window: We added a head to predict which needs will appear in the next 30 days, rather than just the next single intent .
  • Life Events Mapping: 30-day needs roll up into macro life events (e.g., baby, car, home, bike, investing) .
  • KBC Format: The KBC sample JSON goes straight into our data flattening pipeline .
  • Actionable Evidence: The decoder's cross-attention directly names the events behind each prediction (e.g., `transaction.mcc=8011` for a gynaecologist) .

Limitations & Path to Production

Current Limitations:

  • All numbers come from synthetic training data, which proves the method works but doesn't measure it directly on KBC customers .
  • Transfer to bare transactions is uneven; customers with few transactions get weak predictions once a need is met .

Next Steps with Real Data:

  • Train on real KBC intent labels (app actions, advisor notes, product applications) .
  • Add memo text and integrate additional channels like call centres and branches .
  • Run the paper's ablations on real data to see which parts matter most .
